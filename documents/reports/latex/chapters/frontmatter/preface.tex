\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
This thesis presents a pipeline for fine-tuning a local large language model (LLM) to detect Vietnamese financial phishing messages while preserving user privacy through on-device inference. A reproducible labelled dataset of 3,000 Vietnamese messages is constructed from cybercrime alert seeds combined with synthetic generation and quality judging. A Qwen3-4B model is adapted using quantized low-rank adaptation (QLoRA) and deployed as a GGUF Q8\_0 artifact for CPU-based laptop inference via llama.cpp. A structured decision layer returns a risk tier, threat label, grounded evidence cues, and actionable safety recommendations for each input. On a 254-example internal holdout (synthetic-derived, seed-disjoint from training), the fine-tuned model yields a macro F1 of 0.9553, compared to 0.670 for the base model without adaptation. All three risky classes exceed 0.87 recall, with task-scam recall at 0.871 on 62 held-out examples. Deployment in safety-critical settings is not recommended without validation on a real-world labelled corpus.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Vietnamese phishing detection; local large language models; explainable artificial intelligence; QLoRA adaptation; GGUF inference; financial fraud prevention.
