This chapter reports the final training, deployment, and evaluation evidence. The quantitative comparison uses the 254-example validation split and must not be interpreted as an independent test result. Appendix~3 links each reported value to its source artifact.

\section{Data Quality and Model Selection}
A combined LLM-as-judge review covered 100 generated examples in two batches of 50 (scored by independent models to reduce single-judge bias). Ninety-four passed, with a combined mean realism score of 4.43 out of 5 ($t = 8.7$, $p < 0.0001$) and a mean label-correctness score of 4.92 out of 5 ($t = 53.2$, $p < 0.0001$), both tested against $H_0\colon \mu \leq 4.0$. Pydantic separately enforces the output schema and permitted score ranges; the LLM judges assess semantic quality.

A recall-first pilot over 33 fixed samples selected \texttt{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507}; \texttt{Qwen3.5-4B} was retained as the alternative candidate. The recorded training job adapted the selected model using 4-bit NF4 QLoRA on 2{,}333 training records, with 254 records supplied for validation. After one epoch, checkpoint 584 recorded a training loss of 0.5073.

\section{Validation Results}
The adapted model correctly classifies 245 of 254 validation messages, giving an accuracy of 0.9646. Bank impersonation reaches precision 0.862, recall 1.000, and F1 0.926 on 56 examples. Zalo social engineering reaches precision 1.000, recall 0.987, and F1 0.993 on 75 examples. Task scam reaches precision 1.000, recall 0.871, and F1 0.931 on 62 examples. Benign reaches 1.000 for all three metrics on 61 examples.

The predefined risky-class recall thresholds were 0.90 for bank impersonation, 0.90 for Zalo social engineering, and 0.80 for task scam. All three observed recalls meet their respective thresholds. These thresholds are development criteria rather than production safety guarantees.

\subsection*{Error Analysis}
The nine misclassified rows are not scattered noise; they share a specific textual pattern. All eight task-scam errors read, in the message text itself, as a fake bank notice: for example, ``Chào bạn, đây là Ngân hàng ABC... vui lòng đăng nhập vào [www.abc-bank-upgrade.com] để xác nhận thông tin'' and ``Xin chào, tôi là nhân viên hỗ trợ của VietPay... Vui lòng click vào [vietpay-update.com] để kiểm tra và xác nhận lại OTP.'' Both were labeled \texttt{task\_scam} at generation time, yet both name a bank or payment provider, request an OTP or login, and use the same urgency wording that defines the \texttt{bank\_impersonation} class elsewhere in the corpus. The single Zalo error is the same pattern: ``Anh/chị ơi, bên em là Ngân hàng VPBank thông báo... anh/chị vui lòng cung cấp mã OTP...'' is a bank-impersonation-style message that happened to be generated under the Zalo target class. In each case the model's predicted label is a reasonable reading of the text; the error traces to a class-boundary overlap in the synthetic corpus between task-scam or social-engineering messages that adopt bank-notice framing and messages generated directly as bank impersonation, rather than to a model failure to recognize the underlying threat. This distinction matters for the reported metrics: as the binary collapse above shows, all nine errors keep the correct scam/benign decision, and the disagreement is over which scam subtype label best fits a message that legitimately resembles two of them.

\section{Deployment Verification}
The final adapter was merged with \texttt{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507} and exported as a Q8\_0 GGUF file. The artifact is 4{,}280{,}403{,}232 bytes; its SHA-256 begins \texttt{1cc523ce7ec7e51a} and is recorded in full in the evidence manifest. It is registered under the \texttt{gguf-laptop} profile. The runtime doctor check passed, and a CPU-only CLI analysis produced a structured risk tier, threat label, grounded cues, and safety recommendations. This verifies the deployment path independently of the adapter-based validation run.

\section{Limits of the Evidence}
Several factors bound how the reported metrics should be read. Each is stated here directly rather than left for the reader to infer from the numbers alone.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Seed overlap across splits.} The 254-row evaluation split was also used for periodic validation during training, and a documented limitation of the current generation pipeline is that one seed group contributes records to multiple splits. The reported metrics should therefore be read as internal development evidence rather than an independent test estimate; the 413-row test split remains reserved for future benchmarking.
\item \textbf{Small per-class validation support.} Per-class counts range from 56 to 75 examples (Table~\ref{tab:dataset-stats}). This is enough to compare the base and adapted model on the same rows, but a single misclassified example shifts a class's recall by roughly 1-2 percentage points, so the precision of any individual per-class figure is limited by sample size, not just by model quality.
\item \textbf{Generation-time labeling and quality-judge circularity.} Labels are assigned at generation time rather than by independent annotation (Section~\ref{sec:problem-statement}), and the quality-judge pass that screens them shares a model family with the generator for half its sample; Section~\ref{sec:quality-check} reports the batch-level pass-rate gap this produces (98\% self-judged vs.\ 90\% independently judged) rather than treating the combined score as bias-free.
\item \textbf{Single training epoch.} The adapted model was trained for one epoch (Chapter~III), chosen to limit memorization of the synthetic corpus's templated phrasing rather than derived from an epoch sweep; a multi-epoch comparison was not run.
\item \textbf{Class-boundary ambiguity.} The error analysis above shows the nine validation errors cluster on messages whose text legitimately resembles two classes at once, indicating the four-class taxonomy has at least one soft boundary rather than four cleanly separable categories.
\item \textbf{Synthetic-only corpus.} The corpus is Vietnamese-only and entirely synthetic-derived; generalization to real user traffic, other languages, or adversarially-evolved scam phrasing is unverified.
\end{enumerate}

The structured output contract (risk tier, labels, cues, recommendations) is demonstrated at runtime. The semantic quality of explanations would benefit from a dedicated human review in future work. Version~1 remains text-only and synthetic-derived; real-world, human-labelled evaluation is required before operational use~\cite{lim2025explicate}.
