This chapter separates four kinds of evidence that answer different questions: corpus quality, same-cohort development validation, bounded hardware feasibility, and one terminal two-model evaluation. This separation is necessary because a good validation score does not prove dataset realism, a resource probe does not prove accuracy, and one terminal result does not estimate run-to-run variance or production performance.

\section{Final Corpus and Data-Quality Evidence}

The promoted corpus contains 2{,}097 records in four classes. Group-integrity splitting produced 1{,}658 training, 219 validation, and 220 terminal-evaluation rows with zero cross-split \texttt{seed\_id} overlap. The largest seed accounts for 7.9638\% of the corpus, below the predeclared 8\% concentration cap. Table~\ref{tab:dataset-stats} gives both class and partition counts.

The final machine-judge bundle covers all 2{,}097 promoted records by joining 1{,}561 exact-record carries with 536 freshly judged final-delta records. It records 1{,}395 passes (66.52\%). Mean rubric scores on the 1--5 scale are realism 4.020, label correctness 4.851, code-switch naturalness 4.672, risk-tier correctness 4.119, and suspicious-span accuracy 4.745. Semantic convergence ended with zero unresolved rows. These are descriptive quality indicators; the bundle is not described as a new independent judgment of every row because it mixes carried and fresh evidence.

The separate Vietnamese-fluent human sample contains 100 final-snapshot records: 44 PASS and 56 FAIL. Human and judge verdicts agree on 87/100. The sample is stratified across all four labels, both judge outcomes, and both judge-evidence origins; it is partial corroboration rather than complete human annotation. A further targeted review considered all 324 task-scam candidates flagged for possible mislabelling. The reviewer chose 91 drops and approved 233 relabels. Only 57 relabels were admitted; 176 human-approved Zalo semantics were excluded solely because they shared one non-independent lineage. This is evidence that lineage governance was applied even when it reduced the amount of usable reviewed data.

The reconstructed Zalo subset requires a separate independence limitation. The offline reconstruction and final judge share a model family, so the 296 reconstructed records remaining in the corpus are not generator-family-independent judgments. They did pass schema, span, duplicate, label-support, seed-cap, and split-disjointness checks, but those mechanical gates do not turn synthetic messages into observed real-world traffic.

\section{Bounded Resource Evidence}

The same-laptop LoRA and QLoRA probes answer whether each adaptation route was practical on the RTX~5050 configuration. They do not contribute model-quality predictions. Ordinary LoRA completed optimizer work but stopped with \texttt{parent\_controller\_error} after 31 observed events. It peaked at 7{,}902/8{,}151~MiB VRAM, reached 9~MiB minimum free VRAM, and had a 53.274-second median measured step. Its 18.42-hour figure is a compute-only projection from the incomplete 26-step measurement window. No OOM occurred.

The genuine NF4 QLoRA probe completed five warm-up plus 40 measured steps, retained 395~MiB minimum free VRAM, and had a 3.462389-second median. Its 72-minute-49.750-second schedule figure combines a projected 1{,}245-step optimizer schedule with one measured validation and save overhead. It is not the observed duration of the later full run. Figure~\ref{fig:resource-probe-comparison} in Chapter~IV therefore labels both duration values as projections.

The defensible resource conclusion is narrow: ordinary LoRA was technically runnable but had effectively no device-memory headroom and an unattractive deadline estimate, while genuine QLoRA reached its bounded target with more headroom. No full ordinary-LoRA model or accuracy row exists, and no cross-family speed conclusion is drawn from Qwen and PhoBERT execution times.

\section{Preclaim Failures and Provenance Correction}

Five superseded terminal-evaluation attempts failed before a terminal claim and before model evaluation: a repository bootstrap rejected OneDrive reparse ancestry; the staged loader lacked required source-location metadata; the launcher lacked an explicit output-root argument; captured-helper identity depended on unstable marshal reference bytes; and base leases were compared using semantic snapshot hashes instead of their bound tree hashes. Each failure was retained. The final protected authority passed its preflight, created the durable claim, evaluated both models once, and exited successfully. No terminal model-evaluation retry occurred.

The absolute phrase ``zero prior filesystem access'' would nevertheless be false. At least two broad default pytest executions before the terminal model evaluation parsed, statted, and hashed the live split files; the exact number may be higher. One focused post-evaluation regression rerun also reread them. These automated integrity reads did not display row content to a human, run either model, call an external service, or influence training, validation acceptance, checkpoint selection, thresholding, dataset repair, or the terminal result. The frozen result therefore remains exactly one two-model evaluation pass, but it does not have zero prior filesystem access to the evaluation file. The verified export is accompanied by a mandatory corrective erratum rather than being silently resealed.

\section{Limits of the Evidence}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Synthetic-derived corpus.} The messages are synthetic-derived rather than a representative sample of live Vietnamese scam traffic. Controlled generation, repair, deduplication, and span checks improve internal quality but cannot establish external validity \cite{kaddour2023synthetic}.
\item \textbf{Bounded human corroboration.} The 100-row stratified sample and 324-candidate targeted triage are substantive manual checks, but neither is full-corpus annotation. The 44\% human sample pass rate is a warning against treating high judge rubric means as proof of uniformly natural text.
\item \textbf{Judge-family overlap.} The 296 retained reconstructed Zalo rows share a model family with the final judge, so their judge verdicts are not generator-family-independent.
\item \textbf{One seed and one terminal pass.} Each model has one seed-42 run. No epoch/seed sweep, variance estimate, significance test, or stable-superiority claim is supported. PhoBERT's higher terminal metrics are a descriptive result on this cohort.
\item \textbf{Evaluation-file access disclosure.} The terminal models ran once, but automated integrity tests accessed the evaluation file before and after that run as described above. The report therefore makes no absolute prior-isolation or zero-access claim.
\item \textbf{Terminal policy.} The terminal result was not used for retraining, repair, checkpoint reselection, threshold adjustment, or contingency activation. Deployment fitting remains deferred, so there is no claim of a final all-data fit.
\item \textbf{Artifact scope.} Qwen GGUF construction and loadability were verified, but PhoBERT was not converted to GGUF and comparative deployment latency was not established.
\item \textbf{Open architecture review debt.} The maintained refactor still has six critical and three warning findings concerning filesystem/native guard completeness, command-route discovery, loopback request security, terminal escaping, and test-runner controls. These findings do not alter frozen metrics, but they prevent a production-readiness claim.
\item \textbf{Operational generalization.} Version~1 is text-only and Vietnamese-focused. Robustness to screenshots, voice calls, adversarially evolved wording, and real user traffic remains unverified. Dedicated human review of explanation quality is also future work \cite{lim2025explicate}.
\end{enumerate}

Within those limits, the project demonstrates a complete evidence chain: seed-governed data construction, same-laptop resource probes, two fresh full local models, mechanically derived training graphs, verified Qwen GGUF export, one terminal shared-cohort evaluation, and an explicit record of failures and corrective disclosures.
