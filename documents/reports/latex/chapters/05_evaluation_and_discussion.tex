This chapter reports the final training, deployment, and evaluation evidence. The quantitative comparison uses the 254-example validation split and must not be interpreted as an independent test result. Appendix~3 links each reported value to its source artifact.

\section{Data Quality and Model Selection}
A combined LLM-as-judge review covered 100 generated examples in two batches of 50 (scored by independent models to reduce single-judge bias). Ninety-four passed, with a combined mean realism score of 4.43 out of 5 ($t = 8.7$, $p < 0.0001$) and a mean label-correctness score of 4.92 out of 5 ($t = 53.2$, $p < 0.0001$), both tested against $H_0\colon \mu \leq 4.0$. Pydantic separately enforces the output schema and permitted score ranges; the LLM judges assess semantic quality.

A recall-first pilot over 33 fixed samples selected \texttt{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507}; \texttt{Qwen3.5-4B} was retained as the alternative candidate. The recorded training job adapted the selected model using 4-bit NF4 QLoRA on 2{,}333 training records, with 254 records supplied for validation. After one epoch, checkpoint 584 recorded a training loss of 0.5073.

\section{Validation Results}
The adapted model correctly classifies 245 of 254 validation messages, giving an accuracy of 0.9646. Bank impersonation reaches precision 0.862, recall 1.000, and F1 0.926 on 56 examples. Zalo social engineering reaches precision 1.000, recall 0.987, and F1 0.993 on 75 examples. Task scam reaches precision 1.000, recall 0.871, and F1 0.931 on 62 examples. Benign reaches 1.000 for all three metrics on 61 examples.

The predefined risky-class recall thresholds were 0.90 for bank impersonation, 0.90 for Zalo social engineering, and 0.80 for task scam. All three observed recalls meet their respective thresholds. These thresholds are development criteria rather than production safety guarantees.

\section{Deployment Verification}
The final adapter was merged with \texttt{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507} and exported as a Q8\_0 GGUF file. The artifact is 4{,}280{,}403{,}232 bytes; its SHA-256 begins \texttt{1cc523ce7ec7e51a} and is recorded in full in the evidence manifest. It is registered under the \texttt{gguf-laptop} profile. The runtime doctor check passed, and a CPU-only CLI analysis produced a structured risk tier, threat label, grounded cues, and safety recommendations. This verifies the deployment path independently of the adapter-based validation run.

\section{Limits of the Evidence}
The 254-row evaluation split was also used for periodic validation during training. A documented limitation of the current generation pipeline is that one seed group contributes records to multiple splits; the reported metrics should therefore be read as internal development evidence rather than an independent test estimate. The 413-row test split remains reserved for future benchmarking.

The structured output contract (risk tier, labels, cues, recommendations) is demonstrated at runtime. The semantic quality of explanations would benefit from a dedicated human review in future work. Version~1 remains text-only and synthetic-derived; real-world, human-labelled evaluation is required before operational use~\cite{lim2025explicate}.
