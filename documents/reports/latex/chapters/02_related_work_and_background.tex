We review existing work across six areas that shape our design: Vietnamese phishing patterns, LLM-based threat detection, local inference for privacy, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, synthetic data generation, and explainable AI for cybersecurity. We close with the research gap that motivates this project.

\section{Vietnamese Financial Phishing}

Vietnam faces a growing wave of financial phishing through SMS, Zalo, and social media platforms. INTERPOL's 2024 Asia-Pacific cyberthreat assessment reported that 5.5 per 1{,}000 individuals in the region clicked phishing links monthly --- twice the global average --- with Vietnam among the most affected countries \cite{interpol2024cyberthreat}. The National Cyber Security Association of Vietnam estimated VND~18.9 trillion (\$744M) in online fraud losses in 2024, with 66.24\% of surveyed users reporting unauthorized use of personal data \cite{nca2024cybersecurity}. Group-IB documented a targeted campaign that singled out clients of major Vietnamese banks using cloned login pages distributed via SMS \cite{groupib2022laser}, and the Vietnamese authority for information security warned about bank impersonation messages exploiting biometric authentication flows \cite{ais2024biometricwarning}.

Vietnamese phishing text poses specific NLP challenges. Diacritics carry meaning (e.g., \textit{ma} vs \textit{mã} vs \textit{mà}), and informal messages often drop or misplace them. Le-Hong~\cite{lehong2021diacritics} showed that restoring diacritics with deep learning improves downstream social-media classification accuracy. Early work by Pham and Le-Hong~\cite{pham2016vietnamese} applied content-based filtering to Vietnamese spam SMS, but their feature set was limited to bag-of-words and n-grams. PhoBERT~\cite{nguyen2020phobert}, the first large-scale monolingual Vietnamese language model, achieved strong results on multiple Vietnamese NLP tasks including text classification, establishing a pre-trained backbone for Vietnamese downstream work. ViSoBERT~\cite{nguyen2023visobert} extended this to social-media text, outperforming PhoBERT on informal Vietnamese tasks such as spam detection and hate speech classification --- directly relevant to the register of phishing messages.

Despite these advances, no public benchmark dataset exists for Vietnamese financial phishing classification with grounded explanations. The closest resources are generic Vietnamese spam corpora, which do not distinguish threat types (bank impersonation, task scam, social engineering) or provide evidence-level annotation.

\section{LLM-based Phishing and Fraud Detection}

Recent work has applied large language models to phishing detection. Patel et al.~\cite{patel2024evaluating} benchmarked 15 LLMs on phishing email identification using 419-scam datasets, finding GPT-3.5 variants most effective but noting that all models struggled with novel phishing patterns not seen during pre-training. Koide et al.~\cite{koide2024chatspamdetector} proposed ChatSpamDetector, achieving 99.70\% accuracy with GPT-4 while generating natural-language explanations for each detection --- an approach close to ours, though dependent on cloud API access.

A key question for our work is whether fine-tuning outperforms prompting. Trad and Chehab~\cite{trad2024prompt} compared prompt engineering against fine-tuning for phishing URL detection and found that fine-tuned smaller models (97.29\% F1) outperformed prompted large models (92.74\% F1), supporting our decision to fine-tune a 4B model rather than prompt a larger one. Goldenits et al.~\cite{goldenits2025small} evaluated 15 small language models (1B--70B parameters) for local phishing website detection and demonstrated that locally deployed models can serve as viable privacy-preserving alternatives to cloud APIs --- directly validating the local-deployment approach in this thesis.

On the explainability side, Uddin and Sarker~\cite{uddin2024explainable} fine-tuned DistilBERT for phishing email classification and added LIME-based explanations, showing that transformer-based detection can be made interpretable. Xue et al.~\cite{xue2025multiphishguard} proposed MultiPhishGuard, a multi-agent LLM system with an adversarial training loop that improves both detection quality and explanation coverage. Both approaches confirm that combining LLM classification with structured explanations is a productive direction, though neither targets Vietnamese text or local-only inference.

\section{Local Inference as a Privacy Control}

Suspicious financial messages contain names, phone numbers, OTP codes, and account details. Local inference treats this as a privacy control: sensitive text never leaves the device \cite{nist2026privacyframework}. The practical risk of cloud alternatives is not hypothetical. In March 2023, OpenAI disclosed a Redis caching bug that exposed the chat history and payment details of approximately 1.2\% of ChatGPT Plus subscribers \cite{openai2023breach}. In the same year, Samsung engineers pasted confidential chip design source code into ChatGPT, triggering a company-wide ban on generative AI tools \cite{bloomberg2023samsung}.

The viability of on-device LLM inference is now established in industry practice. Google deployed a 4~GB Gemini Nano model to Chrome browsers for local summarization and scam detection without sending content to remote servers \cite{cyberinsider2026geminanano}. Kristiani et al.~\cite{kristiani2026deploying} surveyed model compression, pruning, and quantization strategies for deploying LLMs on edge devices, identifying GGUF-based quantization as a leading approach for CPU-only deployment. Maiya~\cite{maiya2025onprem} released OnPrem.LLM, a privacy-focused toolkit for applying LLMs to sensitive documents in offline environments, supporting llama.cpp as a backend --- the same runtime we use.

The choice of quantization level affects both quality and deployment feasibility. Kurt~\cite{kurt2026quantization} empirically evaluated all llama.cpp GGUF quantization formats on downstream tasks and found that Q8\_0 preserves model quality with minimal measured degradation on downstream benchmarks while reducing file size enough for consumer hardware. We adopt Q8\_0 for our inference artifact based on this evidence.

\section{Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning}

Full fine-tuning of billion-parameter models requires hardware beyond what a typical student or consumer owns. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods address this by updating only a small fraction of model weights. Houlsby et al.~\cite{houlsby2019parameter} introduced adapter modules that achieve within 0.4\% of full fine-tuning performance while adding only 3.6\% of parameters per task --- the foundational idea behind modern PEFT. Hu et al.~\cite{hu2022lora} proposed LoRA, which decomposes weight updates into low-rank matrices ($r \ll d$), eliminating the need to store full gradient copies. Dettmers et al.~\cite{dettmers2023qlora} extended this with QLoRA, combining 4-bit NormalFloat quantization of frozen weights with LoRA adapters trained in full precision, making fine-tuning feasible on a single consumer GPU with as little as 6~GB of VRAM.

Han et al.~\cite{han2024peft} surveyed PEFT methods including adapters, prompt tuning, and prefix tuning. Nwaiwu~\cite{nwaiwu2025peft} compared LoRA, IA3, and ReFT on low-resource text classification, finding that LoRA achieves the highest F1 (0.909) while ReFT delivers 98\% of that performance with only 3\% of the parameters. We select QLoRA for our adaptation because it combines the classification accuracy of LoRA with the memory savings needed to fine-tune on consumer GPU hardware.

\section{Synthetic Data Generation for NLP}

Because no public Vietnamese financial phishing dataset with threat-type labels and grounded evidence annotations currently exists, our training corpus is built entirely through controlled generation: real phishing seeds from NCSC are expanded into full messages using the \texttt{claude-3-5-haiku} API, with automated schema validation and manual quality checks at the generation boundary. Recent work validates synthetic data generation as a practical strategy for low-resource NLP settings.

Kaddour and Liu~\cite{kaddour2023synthetic} showed that synthetic data generated via fine-tuned teacher LLMs produces strong performance gains on text classification in low-resource settings. Long et al.~\cite{long2024synthetic} surveyed LLM-driven synthetic data generation and found that few-shot generation with real examples outperforms zero-shot approaches for classification tasks --- consistent with our seed-based generation design. Li et al.~\cite{li2024datageneration} analyzed how prompt design and data diversity affect synthetic data quality, providing practical recommendations directly applicable to phishing corpus construction. Anikina et al.~\cite{anikina2025rigorous} evaluated LLM data generation across 11 typologically diverse languages and showed that target-language demonstrations narrow the gap with real data to within 5\%, supporting the use of Vietnamese seed examples in our generation pipeline.

We mitigate the known risks of synthetic data through three controls: Pydantic schema validation at generation time, a manual quality-judge pass on 100 random samples with statistical verification ($t$-test, $p < 0.0001$), and seed-disjoint split allocation to prevent train-test leakage within the synthetic domain.

\section{Explainable AI for Cybersecurity}

We treat explainability as a core design requirement rather than a post-hoc addition. Our pipeline returns risk tiers, threat labels, and grounded suspicious cues anchored to the message text itself --- not to opaque confidence scores. Rjoub et al.~\cite{rjoub2023surveyxai} surveyed XAI techniques applied to cybersecurity, identifying a gap between model accuracy and user trust that grounded explanations can address. Sharma et al.~\cite{sharma2025comprehensive} provided a taxonomy of SHAP, LIME, and attention-based explainability methods for security AI, noting that domain-specific evidence grounding remains underexplored.

Lim et al.~\cite{lim2025explicate} proposed EXPLICATE, which grounds phishing explanations in text-level evidence and demonstrated improved user trust compared to confidence-score-only outputs. Their finding that evidence-grounded explanations increase user compliance with security recommendations supports our design choice of returning grounded cues alongside risk tiers.

\section{Research Gap}

The literature shows progress on LLM-based phishing detection, parameter-efficient adaptation, and explainable AI for cybersecurity. No existing work combines all of the following: (1)~a Vietnamese-specific phishing corpus with threat-type labels, (2)~a locally deployed fine-tuned LLM that runs on consumer CPU hardware without cloud dependency, (3)~structured explanations grounded in the message text, and (4)~a recall-first evaluation strategy that prioritizes reducing dangerous false negatives over maximizing overall accuracy. This thesis addresses that gap with a prototype pipeline covering all four requirements.
