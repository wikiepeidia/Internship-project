This chapter reviews existing work across seven areas that shape the system design: Vietnamese phishing patterns, LLM-based threat detection, local inference for privacy, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, generative classification with decoder-only models, synthetic data generation, and explainable AI for cybersecurity. The chapter closes with the research gap that motivates this project.

\section{Vietnamese Financial Phishing}

Vietnamese users encounter financial phishing through SMS, Zalo, and other social platforms. Group-IB documented a campaign targeting customers of Vietnamese banks through cloned login pages distributed by SMS \cite{groupib2022laser}, and the Vietnamese authority for information security warned about bank-impersonation messages exploiting biometric-authentication procedures \cite{ais2024biometricwarning}. These documented examples motivate the threat classes used in this project without requiring a broader national prevalence claim.

Vietnamese phishing text poses specific NLP challenges. Diacritics carry meaning (e.g., \textit{ma} vs \textit{mã} vs \textit{mà}), and informal messages often drop or misplace them. Le-Hong~\cite{lehong2021diacritics} showed that restoring diacritics with deep learning improves downstream social-media classification accuracy. Early work by Pham and Le-Hong~\cite{pham2016vietnamese} applied content-based filtering to Vietnamese spam SMS, but their feature set was limited to bag-of-words and n-grams. PhoBERT~\cite{nguyen2020phobert}, the first large-scale monolingual Vietnamese language model, achieved strong results on multiple Vietnamese NLP tasks including text classification, establishing a pre-trained backbone for Vietnamese downstream work. ViSoBERT~\cite{nguyen2023visobert} extended this to social-media text, outperforming PhoBERT on informal Vietnamese tasks such as spam detection and hate speech classification --- directly relevant to the register of phishing messages. Tran et al.~\cite{tran2022phobertcnn} combined PhoBERT with a Text-CNN head for Vietnamese hate and offensive-content detection on streaming social-media comments, reporting the same informal-register challenges (abbreviations, code-switching, inconsistent diacritics) that phishing messages in this project's corpus also exhibit, and demonstrating that a PhoBERT-based classification head is a viable production pattern for short, informal Vietnamese text --- the same head-based alternative discussed for phishing classification in Section~\ref{sec:qwen-vs-phobert}.

Within the Vietnamese NLP and phishing resources reviewed for this thesis, no public benchmark was found for Vietnamese financial phishing classification with grounded explanations. The closest reviewed resources are generic Vietnamese spam corpora, which do not distinguish threat types (bank impersonation, task scam, social engineering) or provide evidence-level annotation. Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyen2025fakeads} is the closest Vietnamese-domain fraud-detection system found in this review: a multimodal pipeline for detecting fake advertisements on Vietnamese real estate listings, reaching 91.5\% detection accuracy. It targets a different fraud type (listing fraud rather than financial phishing) and a different input modality (multimodal listing data rather than short chat messages), but it confirms that Vietnamese-specific fraud detection is an active area with no financial-phishing-specific counterpart yet published. This is a bounded literature finding rather than proof that no such dataset exists, so two further checks were made to test that boundary directly rather than leave it as an assumption.

First, the seed source itself was quantified rather than assumed to be small. \textit{tinnhiemmang.vn}'s live scam-warning listing was paginated to its terminal page and contains 74 advisory articles in total; this project's retained seed snapshot already traces to 67 of them. There is no large untapped reserve of real cases at this specific source --- synthetic expansion was necessary to reach a training-scale corpus from it, not a shortcut taken for convenience.

Second, one recent candidate dataset was evaluated directly rather than cited on trust. A Vietnamese SMS dataset published on Hugging Face under a CC~BY~4.0 license~\cite{trannguyen2026vnsms} declares 2{,}991 messages with a published train/test split. An independent check of that split --- exact text match after Unicode normalization, case-folding, and whitespace collapse --- found that 25.3\% of the test set's positive (phishing) rows are verbatim duplicates of rows already present in the training set, which would inflate any classifier's reported test performance on the split as published. The dataset's documentation was also revised four times within three days of upload without the underlying data files changing. Because of the confirmed train/test leakage, this dataset was not adopted into this project's corpus or evaluation; a usable subset would require independent re-labeling, re-splitting, and its own authenticity review before any future use, not just a license check.

\section{LLM-based Phishing and Fraud Detection}

Recent work has applied large language models to phishing detection. Patel et al.~\cite{patel2024evaluating} benchmarked 15 LLMs on phishing email identification using 419-scam datasets, finding GPT-3.5 variants most effective but noting that all models struggled with novel phishing patterns not seen during pre-training. Koide et al.~\cite{koide2024chatspamdetector} proposed ChatSpamDetector, achieving 99.70\% accuracy with GPT-4 while generating natural-language explanations for each detection --- an approach close to ours, though dependent on cloud API access.

A key question for this work is whether fine-tuning outperforms prompting. Trad and Chehab~\cite{trad2024prompt} compared prompt engineering against fine-tuning for phishing URL detection and found that fine-tuned smaller models (97.29\% F1) outperformed prompted large models (92.74\% F1), supporting the decision to fine-tune a 4B model rather than prompt a larger one. Goldenits et al.~\cite{goldenits2026small} evaluated 15 small language models (1B--70B parameters) for local phishing website detection and demonstrated that locally deployed models can serve as privacy-preserving alternatives to cloud APIs, which supports the local-deployment direction examined in this thesis.

On the explainability side, Uddin and Sarker~\cite{uddin2024explainable} fine-tuned DistilBERT for phishing email classification and added LIME-based explanations, showing that transformer-based detection can be made interpretable. Xue et al.~\cite{xue2025multiphishguard} proposed MultiPhishGuard, a multi-agent LLM system with an adversarial training loop that improves both detection quality and explanation coverage. Both approaches support combining model classification with structured explanations, though neither targets Vietnamese text or local-only inference.

Closer to this project's threat model, Hossain et al.~\cite{hossain2025scambaiting} proposed a privacy-preserving, LLM-based real-time scam-detection system that keeps conversation content under federated rather than centralized processing, motivated by the same privacy concern that motivates this project's local-inference boundary (Section~\ref{sec:local-privacy}). Their system targets live conversational scambaiting rather than single-message classification with grounded evidence output, so the problem framing differs, but the shared privacy motivation is a direct precedent for treating on-device or non-centralized processing as a requirement rather than an optimization.

\section{Local Inference as a Privacy Control}
\label{sec:local-privacy}

Suspicious financial messages contain names, phone numbers, OTP codes, and account details. The NIST Privacy Framework motivates identifying and limiting privacy risks in data processing \cite{nist2026privacyframework}; this project implements that principle by keeping inference on the device. The practical risk of cloud alternatives is not hypothetical. In March 2023, OpenAI disclosed a Redis caching bug that exposed the chat history and payment details of approximately 1.2\% of ChatGPT Plus subscribers \cite{openai2023breach}. In the same year, Samsung engineers pasted confidential chip design source code into ChatGPT, triggering a company-wide ban on generative AI tools \cite{bloomberg2023samsung}.

Several systems demonstrate the feasibility of on-device LLM inference. Google deployed a 4~GB Gemini Nano model to Chrome browsers for local summarization and scam detection without sending content to remote servers \cite{cyberinsider2026geminanano}. Kristiani et al.~\cite{kristiani2026deploying} surveyed model compression, pruning, and quantization strategies for deploying LLMs on edge devices, identifying GGUF-based quantization as one approach for CPU-only deployment. Maiya~\cite{maiya2025onprem} released OnPrem.LLM, a privacy-focused toolkit for applying LLMs to sensitive documents in offline environments, supporting llama.cpp as a backend --- the runtime also used in this project.

The choice of quantization level affects both quality and deployment feasibility. Kurt~\cite{kurt2026quantization} empirically evaluated all llama.cpp GGUF quantization formats on downstream tasks and found that Q8\_0 preserves model quality with minimal measured degradation on downstream benchmarks while reducing file size enough for consumer hardware. The Q8\_0 format is adopted for the inference artifact based on this evidence.

\section{Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning}

Full fine-tuning of billion-parameter models requires hardware beyond what a typical student or consumer owns. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods address this by updating only a small fraction of model weights. Houlsby et al.~\cite{houlsby2019parameter} introduced adapter modules that achieve within 0.4\% of full fine-tuning performance while adding only 3.6\% of parameters per task --- the foundational idea behind modern PEFT. Hu et al.~\cite{hu2022lora} proposed LoRA, which decomposes weight updates into low-rank matrices ($r \ll d$), eliminating the need to store full gradient copies. Dettmers et al.~\cite{dettmers2023qlora} extended this with QLoRA, combining 4-bit NormalFloat quantization of frozen weights with LoRA adapters trained in full precision, making fine-tuning feasible on a single consumer GPU with as little as 6~GB of VRAM.

Han et al.~\cite{han2024peft} surveyed PEFT methods including adapters, prompt tuning, and prefix tuning. Nwaiwu~\cite{nwaiwu2025peft} compared LoRA, IA3, and ReFT on low-resource text classification, finding that LoRA achieves the highest F1 (0.909) while ReFT delivers 98\% of that performance with only 3\% of the parameters. QLoRA is selected for this project's adaptation because it combines the classification accuracy of LoRA with the memory savings needed to fine-tune on consumer GPU hardware.

\section{Generative Classification with Decoder-Only Models}

A PEFT-adapted model still needs a mechanism for turning its output into a class decision, and the literature offers two established routes. The first attaches a classification head to the model's hidden representation and trains that head directly; this is the older, more common pattern and the one PhoBERT (Section~\ref{sec:qwen-vs-phobert}) uses. The second reframes classification itself as text generation. Raffel et al.~\cite{raffel2020t5} showed with T5 that a single text-to-text objective can subsume classification, translation, and summarization alike, with the class name generated as output text rather than read off a fixed-size output layer. Schick and Sch{\"u}tze~\cite{schick2021pet} formalized the class-name-as-text mechanism as a \textit{verbalizer}: a fixed mapping from each class to a literal string the model is trained to produce, which is the same mechanism this project's fine-tuned decoder uses for its four threat labels (Section~\ref{sec:qwen-vs-phobert}). Narang et al.~\cite{narang2020wt5} extended this to joint label-and-explanation generation with WT5 and found the joint form more faithful than generating an explanation for a separately predicted label. Most directly relevant, Yousefiramandi and Cooney~\cite{yousefiramandi2025finetuning} compared an embedding-based classification head against instruction-based generative classification on the same causal decoder-only base model, giving a direct precedent for the choice examined in this project rather than an analogy drawn from an encoder architecture.

\section{Synthetic Data Generation for NLP}

Within the sources reviewed for this project, no public Vietnamese financial phishing dataset was found that combines threat-type labels with grounded evidence annotations. The training corpus was therefore built through controlled generation: real phishing seeds from NCSC were expanded into full messages using Claude 3.5 Haiku, with automated schema validation at the generation boundary. Final quality governance subsequently joined Codex judgments for all 2{,}097 promoted records through 1{,}561 exact-record carries and 536 fresh final-delta judgments, then added a separate 100-row human sample. Recent work supports synthetic data generation as a practical strategy for low-resource NLP settings.

Kaddour and Liu~\cite{kaddour2023synthetic} showed that synthetic data generated via fine-tuned teacher LLMs produces strong performance gains on text classification in low-resource settings. Long et al.~\cite{long2024synthetic} surveyed LLM-driven synthetic data generation and found that few-shot generation with real examples outperforms zero-shot approaches for classification tasks --- consistent with the seed-based generation design used here. Li et al.~\cite{li2024datageneration} analyzed how prompt design and data diversity affect synthetic data quality, providing practical recommendations directly applicable to phishing corpus construction. Anikina et al.~\cite{anikina2025rigorous} evaluated LLM data generation across 11 typologically diverse languages and showed that target-language demonstrations narrow the gap with real data to within 5\%, supporting the use of Vietnamese seed examples in the generation pipeline.

The known risks of synthetic data are addressed through Pydantic schema validation, joined semantic-review evidence, the manifest-bound human sample, lineage governance, and explicit provenance fields. The promoted train, validation, and test partitions have zero cross-split seed leakage. Seed overlap remains a limitation only of the earlier 254-row historical model-validation snapshot, whose model metrics were not recomputed on the promoted corpus.

\section{Explainable AI for Cybersecurity}

Explainability is treated as a core design requirement rather than a post-hoc addition. The pipeline returns risk tiers, threat labels, and grounded suspicious cues anchored to the message text itself --- not to opaque confidence scores. Rjoub et al.~\cite{rjoub2023surveyxai} surveyed XAI techniques applied to cybersecurity, identifying a gap between model accuracy and user trust that grounded explanations can address. Sharma et al.~\cite{sharma2025comprehensive} provided a taxonomy of SHAP, LIME, and attention-based explainability methods for security AI, noting that domain-specific evidence grounding remains underexplored.

Lim et al.~\cite{lim2025explicate} proposed EXPLICATE, which grounds phishing explanations in text-level evidence and demonstrated improved user trust compared to confidence-score-only outputs. Their finding that evidence-grounded explanations increase user compliance with security recommendations supports this project's design choice of returning grounded cues alongside risk tiers.

\section{Research Gap}

The comparison shows progress on LLM-based phishing detection, parameter-efficient adaptation, and explainable AI for cybersecurity. None of the systems reviewed here combines all of the following: (1)~a Vietnamese-specific phishing corpus with threat-type labels, (2)~a locally deployed fine-tuned LLM that runs on consumer CPU hardware without cloud dependency, (3)~structured explanations grounded in the message text, and (4)~a recall-first evaluation strategy that prioritizes reducing dangerous false negatives over maximizing overall accuracy. This thesis studies that combination through a prototype pipeline.
