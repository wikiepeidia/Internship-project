Vietnamese financial phishing increasingly appears through SMS and chat platforms such as Zalo, Messenger, Telegram, and Facebook. In that setting, suspicious messages often contain sensitive banking details, phone numbers, one-time passwords, or account recovery prompts \cite{ais2024biometricwarning,groupib2022laser}. This thesis studies a local-first phishing detection pipeline designed to help users inspect such text without sending raw content to cloud services by default.

\section{Background and Motivation}
The thesis follows three design constraints from the beginning of the project: the system is text-only, inference is offline or local by default, and the design prioritizes recall on high-harm scam classes. These choices narrow the scope compared to a full multimodal fraud assistant, but they keep the project realistic for consumer hardware and well-suited for privacy-sensitive message analysis. They also address a practical safety question: how to reduce dangerous false negatives without relying on black-box predictions.

\section{Problem Statement}
Two practical problems motivate the work. First, generic cloud-based fraud checking workflows require users to upload suspicious financial messages to remote services, which creates avoidable privacy exposure. Second, generic multilingual models do not reliably encode Vietnamese financial scam patterns, local social-engineering tactics, or mixed Vietnamese-English code-switching. The project therefore targets a localized explainable pipeline that can classify suspicious text locally and return clear, grounded guidance instead of vague alerts.
