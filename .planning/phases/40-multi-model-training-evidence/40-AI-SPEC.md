## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid supervised NLP classification: Qwen generative structured classification plus PhoBERT encoder classification

**Description:**
This phase trains and documents three local/self-hosted Vietnamese financial-message classifiers: matched Qwen LoRA and genuine 4-bit QLoRA runs plus a PhoBERT sequence-classification baseline. Good means the runs are independently reproducible, use only the frozen training and validation partitions, preserve auditable raw evidence, and support honest comparison without touching the reserved test partition.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. **Evaluation contamination:** any Phase 40 code reads the reserved 220-row test partition, or a `seed_id`/semantic root crosses train and validation. Either event invalidates model selection evidence rather than merely lowering its quality.
2. **Dangerous safe prediction:** a bank-impersonation, Zalo social-engineering, or task-scam message is predicted `benign`, or malformed/unknown Qwen output is silently coerced to `benign` instead of remaining an explicit invalid output.
3. **Hidden class collapse:** macro or weighted scores look acceptable while one risky class has poor recall, especially the smaller 22-row Zalo validation support, or systematic bank/Zalo/task boundary confusions are omitted.
4. **Benign-boundary overreach:** ordinary Vietnamese financial or security notices are labeled as scams merely because they mention a bank, OTP, Zalo, a link, money, or a job. This produces measurable benign-to-risky errors and undermines later user trust.
5. **Unverifiable experiment claims:** a run is named LoRA, QLoRA, or PhoBERT without proving its actual mode/configuration, or metrics and graphs cannot be rebuilt from retained raw logs tied to the canonical train/validation hashes.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Vietnamese retail-finance fraud prevention and consumer cybersecurity research

**User Population:** In Phase 40, the immediate users are the student researcher, supervisors, and technical reviewers choosing and freezing three candidate checkpoints from development evidence. The later product population is Vietnamese users checking SMS, Zalo, and other financial messages; Phase 40 does not yet make a deployment or held-out performance claim.

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** Validation results determine which checkpoints and configurations advance to the one-time Phase 41 held-out evaluation. A false safe prediction in a later user-facing system could expose a person to credential theft or financial loss, while a false alarm on a legitimate notice can cause distrust or needless disruption. Phase 40 therefore measures these risks on validation only and leaves every final generalization claim to Phase 41.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Harmful-scam catch rate by family**
**Good (domain expert would accept):** The 219-row validation report shows support, recall, precision, and F1 separately for `bank_impersonation` (76 rows), `zalo_social_engineering` (22), and `task_scam` (49), plus every risky-to-`benign` error. The current development policy floors (0.90, 0.90, and 0.80 recall respectively) are applied as predeclared validation criteria, not presented as held-out results.
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Only macro/weighted F1 is shown, a weak class is hidden by larger classes, an invalid generated answer is counted as benign, or the development result is described as final test performance.
**Stakes:** Critical
**Source:** Project recall-first policy in `src/model_adaptation/schemas.py`; State Bank of Vietnam and VNCERT/CC warnings describe credential/OTP theft and financial loss from missed scams.

**Dimension: Correct scam mechanism, not channel keywords**
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Confusion-matrix review distinguishes (a) claimed bank authority plus credential, OTP, transfer, fake-app, or fake-site pressure; (b) Zalo/account/relationship or platform-trust abuse, including compromised contacts and fake Zalo partnerships; and (c) job/commission/task funnels that condition payout or withdrawal on deposits, top-ups, order payments, or added fees. A message sent through Zalo is not automatically a Zalo-class scam, and a transfer request is not automatically bank impersonation.
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Labels follow surface tokens such as a bank name, “Zalo,” “OTP,” or “chuyển khoản,” causing systematic bank↔Zalo↔task swaps.
**Stakes:** High
**Source:** State Bank of Vietnam scam guidance; Vietnam information-security authority/VNCERT/CC task-scam warnings; official Zalo Help impersonation and malicious-link guidance; project four-label schema.

**Dimension: Benign-message restraint**
**Good (domain expert would accept):** The report includes benign support (72 validation rows), benign precision/recall, and all benign-to-risky errors. Legitimate transaction notices, official security education, and warnings such as “do not share OTP” remain benign unless the message itself performs a deceptive solicitation.
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Keyword-only behavior marks routine bank or account-security language as malicious, or benign errors are omitted because false negatives are the primary safety concern.
**Stakes:** High
**Source:** Official bank and Zalo guidance uses many of the same surface terms as scams; the project schema makes `benign` a first-class fourth label.

**Dimension: Lineage-independent development evidence**
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Before training, code proves the canonical train and validation identities, zero cross-split `seed_id` overlap, and group-level separation of variants from one semantic root. Metrics always include per-class support and are regenerated from the frozen validation rows only.
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Paraphrases or variants from one root appear on both sides of the split, narrator/scaffold artifacts make labels trivially recognizable, duplicate rows inflate support, or the reserved test is consulted during selection.
**Stakes:** Critical
**Source:** Phase 39 downstream data contract and report note; established project split-governance policy.

**Dimension: Auditable model comparison**
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Each validation number is tied to one immutable checkpoint hash, model revision, exact resolved configuration, raw prediction/log artifact, label order, and canonical split hash. LoRA and QLoRA differ only by the declared quantization treatment, and every graph is reproducible from retained events.
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A graph has no raw source, modes or hardware are mislabeled, configuration differences are hidden, or speed differences across unlike GPUs are treated as model-quality evidence.
**Stakes:** High
**Source:** Phase 40 training-evidence requirements TRAIN-01 through TRAIN-06 and the frozen phase context.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Security-language camouflage:** bank impersonators frame fake links, apps, QR codes, credential requests, or OTP requests as urgent account protection. A detector that treats reassuring language as benign can miss the harmful action.
2. **Actor/channel/mechanism confusion:** a compromised friend on Zalo may request a transfer, and a task recruiter may move a victim to Zalo or mention a bank account. Channel and payment words alone do not identify the scam family; the claimed identity and requested action do.
3. **Task-scam escalation blindness:** “việc nhẹ lương cao” funnels can begin with a plausible job and small reward, then require increasingly large order payments, deposits, or withdrawal fees. Evaluating only the first harmless-looking sentence or only explicit bank terms creates task-scam false negatives.
4. **Synthetic shortcut learning:** narrator framing, repeated generation scaffolds, near-duplicates, or one root split across train/validation can make validation look strong without learning Vietnamese scam semantics. The Phase 39 reconstruction history makes this a demonstrated project risk, not a hypothetical one.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Direct Hugging Face stack: Transformers Trainer + PEFT + Accelerate + PyTorch; bitsandbytes for QLoRA only

**Version:** Transformers 5.9.0; PEFT 0.19.1; Accelerate 1.13.0; PyTorch 2.12.0+cu132; bitsandbytes must be pinned after a compatible version is proven

**Rationale:**
The repository already implements Qwen training with Trainer, PEFT, `LoraConfig`, and `BitsAndBytesConfig`. Extending that stack preserves experimental control, supports matched LoRA/QLoRA settings and PhoBERT sequence classification, exposes raw trainer state, and permits fail-closed quantization without adding orchestration abstractions.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Custom PyTorch loops + PEFT/bitsandbytes | Maximum instrumentation control but higher implementation and reproducibility risk; retain only as fallback if Trainer callbacks cannot capture required evidence. |
| TRL `SFTTrainer` for Qwen | Adds a second training abstraction and does not cover PhoBERT cleanly. |
| Agent/RAG frameworks | LangChain, LangGraph, CrewAI, LlamaIndex, and similar tools solve orchestration or retrieval, not controlled supervised fine-tuning experiments. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No — local open checkpoints and standard Hugging Face artifacts remain portable.

### Entry Point Pattern
```python
from enum import StrEnum

import torch
from peft import LoraConfig, get_peft_model, prepare_model_for_kbit_training
from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM, BitsAndBytesConfig

QWEN_ID = "Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507"
QWEN_REVISION = "cdbee75f17c01a7cc42f958dc650907174af0554"
TARGETS = [
    "q_proj", "k_proj", "v_proj", "o_proj",
    "gate_proj", "up_proj", "down_proj",
]


class QwenMode(StrEnum):
    LORA = "lora"
    QLORA = "qlora"


def build_qwen_adapter(mode: QwenMode):
    """Construct one explicit mode; a QLoRA request either proves 4-bit or raises."""
    if not torch.cuda.is_available():
        raise RuntimeError("Qwen adapter training requires CUDA for Phase 40")
    dtype = torch.bfloat16 if torch.cuda.is_bf16_supported() else torch.float16
    load_kwargs = {
        "revision": QWEN_REVISION,
        "local_files_only": True,
        "trust_remote_code": False,
        "low_cpu_mem_usage": True,
    }

    if mode is QwenMode.QLORA:
        import bitsandbytes as bnb

        load_kwargs.update(
            quantization_config=BitsAndBytesConfig(
                load_in_4bit=True,
                bnb_4bit_quant_type="nf4",
                bnb_4bit_use_double_quant=True,
                bnb_4bit_compute_dtype=dtype,
            ),
            device_map={"": 0},
        )
    else:
        load_kwargs["torch_dtype"] = dtype

    model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(QWEN_ID, **load_kwargs)
    if mode is QwenMode.QLORA:
        linear4_count = sum(isinstance(module, bnb.nn.Linear4bit) for module in model.modules())
        if not getattr(model, "is_loaded_in_4bit", False) or linear4_count == 0:
            raise RuntimeError("QLoRA requested but the loaded model is not genuinely 4-bit")
        model = prepare_model_for_kbit_training(model, use_gradient_checkpointing=True)

    adapter = get_peft_model(
        model,
        LoraConfig(
            r=16,
            lora_alpha=32,
            lora_dropout=0.05,
            bias="none",
            task_type="CAUSAL_LM",
            target_modules=TARGETS,
        ),
    )
    unexpected = [
        name for name, parameter in adapter.named_parameters()
        if parameter.requires_grad and "lora_" not in name
    ]
    if unexpected:
        raise RuntimeError(f"Non-LoRA parameters are trainable: {unexpected[:5]}")
    return adapter
```

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `TrainingArguments` | The immutable resolved run controls consumed by `Trainer` | Pin seed, batch/accumulation, optimizer/scheduler, eval/save/log cadence, precision, and output directory for every run. |
| `Trainer` + `TrainerCallback` | The training/evaluation loop and read-only event hooks | Train both model families; collect optimizer-step timings, raw `log_history`, evaluation events, and CUDA memory without replacing the loop. |
| `LoraConfig` / `PeftModel` | Low-rank trainable adapters over a frozen Qwen base | Apply the identical rank, alpha, dropout, and target modules in both LoRA and QLoRA. |
| `BitsAndBytesConfig` + `prepare_model_for_kbit_training` | 4-bit NF4 base loading and preparation for adapter training | QLoRA only, after the runtime passes the explicit compatibility and 4-bit proof gate. |
| `AutoModelForSequenceClassification` | Encoder plus a four-class classification head | Fully fine-tune `vinai/phobert-base-v2` as the non-generative baseline. |

### Common Pitfalls
1. **Silent pseudo-QLoRA:** the current `_resolve_quantization_config()` returns ordinary LoRA
   when CUDA, bitsandbytes, or `BitsAndBytesConfig` is unavailable. A requested QLoRA run must
   raise before loading the model unless every 4-bit proof passes.
2. **Training on the prompt itself:** the current `DataCollatorForLanguageModeling(mlm=False)`
   labels the whole instruction/prompt sequence. Build labels explicitly and set prompt and
   padding positions to `-100`, so Qwen loss is computed on the assistant JSON response only.
3. **Unmatched comparisons:** changing seed, order, effective batch, sequence length, prompt,
   LoRA targets, evaluation cadence, or hardware alongside quantization invalidates the
   LoRA-versus-QLoRA attribution. Lock and diff the resolved configs before either full run.
4. **Evidence that looks complete but is not:** `Trainer` callbacks can observe state but cannot
   rewrite the training loop. Synchronize CUDA before timing, retain `trainer_state.json` and
   raw logs, and test any narrow `Trainer` override against exactly Transformers 5.9.0.
5. **PhoBERT preprocessing drift:** `vinai/phobert-base-v2` expects word-segmented Vietnamese and
   has a 256-token model limit. Pin one segmentation function/version, hash its output policy,
   and measure truncation; do not feed a raw-text pipeline and call it a matched baseline.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**

- **Qwen base:** `Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507`, revision
  `cdbee75f17c01a7cc42f958dc650907174af0554`. Both full runs start fresh from
  this revision. Ordinary LoRA loads unquantized base weights in BF16 when supported (FP16
  otherwise); QLoRA loads NF4 4-bit base weights with double quantization and the same compute
  dtype. Only LoRA parameters are trainable in either run.
- **Matched adapter controls:** `r=16`, `lora_alpha=32`, `lora_dropout=0.05`, `bias="none"`,
  `task_type="CAUSAL_LM"`, and the repository's seven projection targets (`q/k/v/o`,
  `gate/up/down`). Dataset bytes/order, prompt formatter, response-only mask, seed, data seed,
  maximum length, effective batch, epochs/steps, optimizer, learning-rate schedule, warm-up,
  evaluation/save/log cadence, and validation decoder are identical. The only intended
  treatment difference is base-weight quantization.
- **Qwen starting run controls:** preserve the current `max_seq_length=1024`, per-device batch
  1, gradient accumulation 4, learning rate `2e-4`, logging every 10 optimizer steps, and
  evaluation/checkpoint every 50 steps unless a pre-run plan changes them once for both modes.
  Set `seed=42` and `data_seed=42`. Validation generation is deterministic:
  `do_sample=False`, one sequence, and `max_new_tokens=256`.
- **PhoBERT baseline:** `vinai/phobert-base-v2`, pinned to a full immutable revision before
  download (the known safetensors revision `e966aac8cb889325e073aa5f28ff70aca4dbc8c3`
  is acceptable). Use `AutoModelForSequenceClassification` with the locked label order
  `bank_impersonation`, `zalo_social_engineering`, `task_scam`, `benign`; fully train the
  encoder and head. Start with max length 256, dynamic padding, batch 16 (or the largest value
  proven by the target accelerator), three epochs, learning rate `2e-5`, and the same seeds.
  Its hyperparameters are a separately frozen encoder configuration, not forced to equal Qwen's.
- **Probe boundary:** laptop LoRA and QLoRA probes collect 30-50 post-warm-up optimizer steps
  when their requested mode can genuinely start. They write evidence outside the model registry,
  then their adapters are discarded. OOM is observed and recorded, never engineered or claimed
  in advance.

**Core Pattern:**

1. Before importing/loading a model, resolve both supplied split paths and require that they are
   exactly the canonical `data/splits/train.jsonl` and `data/splits/val.jsonl`. Reject every
   other path before opening it. Verify train SHA/count
   `5fa46382db8fb477ef91ec4ba770bf3f8756df9f98b9950fdf5bc1f6ff402e8b` / 1,658 and
   validation SHA/count
   `746ae6edb5008a8be8e9ef9d65f89fc44e559f99f28cd8d6a77f203ea5986d3c` / 219 against
   `39-DOWNSTREAM-DATA-CONTRACT.json`. Phase 40 code has no test-data parameter and does not
   open an unapproved path merely to identify it.
2. Preserve the existing `format_training_prompt()` response schema, but put system instructions,
   raw message data, and assistant response in distinct Qwen chat-template roles. Fence raw text
   as data, hash the canonical formatter, and tokenize prompt and response separately. Labels are
   `-100` for prompt and padding tokens and real token IDs only for the assistant JSON response.
3. Resolve an explicit mode. `lora` proves there are zero `Linear4bit` base modules. `qlora`
   proves the accepted bitsandbytes pin, CUDA kernel operation, `is_loaded_in_4bit=True`, at least
   one `bnb.nn.Linear4bit`, NF4/double-quant configuration, frozen base parameters, LoRA-only
   trainables, and a successful forward/backward with non-null LoRA gradients. Any failed proof
   aborts before the probe/full run and produces no adapter.
4. Build `TrainingArguments` with explicit seeds, `eval_strategy="steps"`, matching eval/save
   cadence, `report_to="none"`, and `include_num_input_tokens_seen="non_padding"`. Register a
   callback that synchronizes CUDA around optimizer-step timing, excludes warm-up steps from the
   steady-state median, appends each log/eval/save event to JSONL, and captures peak allocated and
   reserved bytes using `reset_peak_memory_stats()`, `max_memory_allocated()`, and
   `max_memory_reserved()`.
5. Run validation only. Qwen outputs are parsed through the typed four-class schema; malformed or
   unknown output becomes an explicit `invalid_output`, never `benign`. PhoBERT logits use argmax
   over the same label order. Both feed one shared macro/weighted/per-class metric implementation.
6. Save `trainer_state.json`, resolved config, sanitized command, package/CUDA/GPU inventory,
   dataset/model/prompt hashes, raw events, final validation metrics, and artifact hashes before
   deriving graphs. Every curve is rebuilt only from the retained raw event file.

`Phase40EvidenceCallback` may observe/log state but must not change losses or control flow. CUDA is
asynchronous, so it calls `torch.cuda.synchronize()` immediately before and after timed regions.
Use Trainer-reported `eval_runtime` for evaluation overhead. If isolated checkpoint duration
requires a `Trainer` override, keep it narrow and lock its method signature with a Transformers
5.9.0 test; otherwise report combined save/evaluation overhead rather than a false precision.

**Tool Use:**

- Transformers/PEFT/Accelerate/PyTorch execute locally or in Colab; bitsandbytes is loaded only by
  the QLoRA factory. There is no web API, hosted inference, external experiment tracker, or secret.
- Pydantic validates the run evidence; `hashlib.sha256` identifies inputs/artifacts; scikit-learn
  computes the common four-class validation metrics; Matplotlib (or the existing graph utility)
  renders curves from JSONL. `report_to="none"` prevents implicit telemetry integrations.
- Capture `python --version`, package versions, `torch.version.cuda`, cuDNN, GPU name/capability,
  total VRAM, precision flags, and a sanitized CLI argument list. Never serialize environment
  variables, tokens, notebook secrets, or absolute personal paths.
- Use `accelerate` only through Trainer's device/distributed integration for these single-process
  runs. Do not introduce DeepSpeed/FSDP or `device_map="auto"` as an extra experimental variable.

**State Management:**

Each run gets an immutable `run_id` directory. Write raw events append-only; write JSON evidence
to a temporary sibling, validate it with Pydantic, `fsync`, atomically replace the target, then
read and validate it once more. A full run may resume only when dataset hashes, model revision,
mode and quantization proof, prompt hash, label order, seeds, and every optimizer/scheduler control
match the checkpoint's resolved config. A mismatch starts a new run. Probe runs never update the
model registry; full-run registry publication occurs only after evidence and artifact hashes pass.

**Context Window Strategy:**

For Qwen, retain the repository's 1,024-token cap and record prompt, response, and total token
length distributions before training. Unexpected truncation is a blocking preflight failure;
if truncation is deliberately accepted later, the count and policy become part of both matched
configs. Mask prompt tokens from loss and set an explicit 256-token validation-generation budget.
For PhoBERT, word-segment raw Vietnamese deterministically, preserve the untouched raw text for
audit/span checks, and cap model input at its 256-token limit. Record how many rows truncate and
never silently use Qwen's 1,024-token setting for the encoder.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice.

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

```python
from typing import Annotated, Literal

from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, Field, StringConstraints, model_validator

Sha256 = Annotated[str, StringConstraints(pattern=r"^[0-9a-f]{64}$")]


class SplitIdentity(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    logical_name: Literal["train", "val"]
    relative_path: str = Field(min_length=1)
    records: int = Field(gt=0)
    sha256: Sha256


class QuantizationProof(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    requested_mode: Literal["lora", "qlora", "phobert"]
    resolved_mode: Literal["full-precision-lora", "4bit-qlora", "full-phobert"]
    bitsandbytes_version: str | None = None
    is_loaded_in_4bit: bool = False
    linear4bit_modules: int = Field(default=0, ge=0)
    kbit_preparation_applied: bool = False
    backward_with_adapter_gradients: bool = False

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def prove_requested_mode(self):
        if self.requested_mode == "qlora":
            if self.resolved_mode != "4bit-qlora":
                raise ValueError("QLoRA may not resolve to another mode")
            if not (
                self.bitsandbytes_version
                and self.is_loaded_in_4bit
                and self.linear4bit_modules > 0
                and self.kbit_preparation_applied
                and self.backward_with_adapter_gradients
            ):
                raise ValueError("Incomplete genuine 4-bit QLoRA proof")
        elif self.is_loaded_in_4bit or self.linear4bit_modules:
            raise ValueError("Non-QLoRA runs must not contain 4-bit modules")
        return self


class RunEvidence(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    schema_version: Literal["phase40-run-evidence-v1"]
    run_id: str = Field(min_length=1)
    run_kind: Literal["probe", "full"]
    model_id: str = Field(min_length=1)
    model_revision: str = Field(pattern=r"^[0-9a-f]{40}$")
    splits: tuple[SplitIdentity, SplitIdentity]
    seed: int
    data_seed: int
    resolved_config_sha256: Sha256
    prompt_or_preprocessor_sha256: Sha256
    package_versions: dict[str, str]
    quantization: QuantizationProof
    peak_allocated_bytes: int = Field(ge=0)
    peak_reserved_bytes: int = Field(ge=0)
    steady_step_seconds_median: float | None = Field(default=None, gt=0)
    validation_metrics: dict[str, float]
    artifact_sha256: dict[str, Sha256]

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def enforce_train_validation_only(self):
        if [split.logical_name for split in self.splits] != ["train", "val"]:
            raise ValueError("Phase 40 evidence must contain train then val only")
        return self
```

The schema records the result of deterministic software, so evidence validation has **zero blind
retries**: a validation error aborts publication and leaves the raw log for diagnosis. The atomic
writer performs one serialize/read-back validation, not a semantic retry. For Qwen validation,
the existing typed classification contract validates each raw generated JSON exactly once; store
the raw output, row identifier, model artifact hash, and validation exception. Schema-invalid or
unknown labels count as `invalid_output` and are never repaired or converted to `benign`. A later
interactive product may allow one logged deterministic regeneration, but that retry is outside
the Phase 40 comparison and must not replace the original validation result.

### Async-First Design

`Trainer.train()` is a synchronous, GPU-bound loop. Run each experiment in its own OS process and,
if an async controller is added, await the subprocess while tailing the append-only event log.
Do not call `asyncio.run()` from a notebook/server event loop, and do not move CUDA work into
multiple Python threads. Stream raw progress for operator visibility; await process completion,
artifact hashing, and Pydantic validation before declaring a run complete. Structured validation
and registry publication are never fire-and-forget.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

Qwen uses one versioned formatter: stable system instruction, explicit output schema, raw message
as delimited user data, and assistant JSON target. Never concatenate untrusted message text into
the instruction namespace. Freeze and hash the exact rendered template for both LoRA and QLoRA.
Use no dynamically retrieved few-shot examples in this controlled experiment; if examples are
later added, lock the same examples and order for both modes and ensure they originate from train
only. Set `max_new_tokens=256`, `do_sample=False`, and one return sequence for validation. PhoBERT
has no prompt; its word-segmentation function and label mapping are the equivalent versioned
preprocessing contract.

### Context Window Management

This is neither RAG nor conversation, so no summarization or retrieval is allowed. Precompute
token-length distributions on the approved train/validation paths, include the formatter overhead,
and block accidental truncation. Qwen uses response-only loss under the 1,024-token cap. PhoBERT
uses deterministic word segmentation and its 256-token limit; record truncated-row identifiers
and rates. Do not split one message into multiple examples because that changes sample weighting
and seed-group semantics.

### Cost and Latency Budget

There is no per-call API charge: training and validation are local/Colab compute, so the monetary
API budget is **$0**. One final Phase 40 validation pass is 219 rows per frozen model, or 657
model-row evaluations across LoRA, QLoRA, and PhoBERT; its external-API cost remains $0. The
resource budget is measured, not guessed. Each laptop probe is capped at
30-50 post-warm-up optimizer steps and reports median steady-state seconds/step, examples and
tokens/second, peak allocated/reserved VRAM, and evaluation/checkpoint overhead. Projected local
runtime equals the measured steady-state median times planned optimizer steps plus measured
overhead and is labeled an estimate. Full Colab runs retain actual wall time and accelerator type;
different GPU types make speed/VRAM comparisons hardware-confounded.

Do not cache training batches or generations across model artifacts in a way that changes measured
work. It is safe to cache deterministic tokenization only when the cache key includes dataset SHA,
model/tokenizer revision, formatter/preprocessor SHA, and maximum length. Validation-generation
cache keys additionally include artifact SHA and decoding config. Prefer deterministic Python
validators and the PhoBERT head for their own fixed task; do not call a cheaper external model for
classification, routing, summarization, or evidence repair. Semantic caching is disabled because
near-duplicate messages remain distinct labeled evaluation rows and merging them would corrupt
support and latency; only exact, fully keyed caches are admissible.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

Phase 40 is a development comparison, not a held-out performance claim. Its only reference set is
the frozen 219-row validation split from the Phase 39 downstream contract. The reserved 220-row
test split is not opened, parsed, hashed, sampled, or used for tuning, checkpoint selection, plots,
or debugging until Phase 41. All three model families use one shared evaluator and the locked label
order `bank_impersonation`, `zalo_social_engineering`, `task_scam`, `benign`.

### Dimensions

| Dimension | Rubric (Pass/Fail or 1-5) | Measurement Approach | Priority |
|-----------|--------------------------|---------------------|----------|
| Validation isolation and lineage | **PASS:** Before model loading, the run accepts only the canonical train and validation paths, proves 1,658/219 rows and their frozen SHA-256 values, proves zero cross-split `seed_id` overlap, and emits no test-data read. **FAIL:** An unapproved path is opened, a hash/count differs, a seed crosses train/validation, or the reserved test is consulted in any Phase 40 decision. | Code, with human evidence review | Critical |
| Structured-output safety | **PASS:** Every Qwen validation row retains raw output and yields either one of the four locked labels or explicit `invalid_output`; malformed JSON, missing/unknown labels, duplicate answers, and parser exceptions are counted as errors and never converted to `benign`. The pass target is zero invalid outputs. **FAIL:** Any invalid output is hidden, repaired post hoc, dropped from the denominator, or treated as safe. | Code | Critical |
| Harmful-scam catch rate by family | **PASS:** The report includes support, precision, recall, and F1 for every class; validation recall is at least 0.90 for `bank_impersonation`, 0.90 for `zalo_social_engineering`, and 0.80 for `task_scam`; every risky-to-`benign` and risky-to-`invalid_output` result is enumerated. **FAIL:** A floor is missed, a harmful miss is omitted, or aggregate scores conceal a weak class. These are development safety gates, not held-out claims. | Code plus human review of every harmful miss | Critical |
| Scam-mechanism discrimination | **PASS:** A fixed confusion matrix exposes bank↔Zalo↔task errors, and error review shows decisions track claimed actor and requested action rather than isolated words such as “Zalo,” “OTP,” or “chuyển khoản.” **FAIL:** Channel or keyword shortcuts cause systematic family swaps, or those swaps are not shown. | Code plus Vietnamese-fluent human review | High |

| Matched LoRA/QLoRA attribution | **PASS:** An automated config diff proves identical Qwen data bytes/order, base revision, formatter and response mask, label order, seeds, sequence length, effective batch, epochs/steps, adapter targets, optimizer/schedule, and log/eval/save cadence; base-weight quantization is the sole intended treatment difference. Cross-GPU speed differences are labeled hardware-confounded. **FAIL:** ETA is presented as completed runtime, OOM is engineered, probe points enter a full curve, or unlike hardware is used for a causal speed claim. | Code plus human interpretation | High |

### Metrics and Selection Contract

- Produce per-class support, precision, recall, and F1, plus macro F1, weighted F1, and accuracy.
  Macro F1 is the primary checkpoint-ranking metric; weighted F1 and accuracy are descriptive only.
- For Qwen, emit a 4-gold-row by 5-prediction-column confusion matrix whose fifth column is
  `invalid_output`. Invalid predictions remain in the denominator and therefore create a false
  negative for the gold class. PhoBERT uses the same schema with a zero `invalid_output` column.
- Select the highest-macro-F1 checkpoint that satisfies all three risky-class recall floors and has
  zero invalid outputs. Break an exact tie by fewer risky-to-`benign` errors, then the earlier step.
  If no checkpoint passes, retain the highest-macro-F1 checkpoint only as a clearly marked
  safety-gate failure for honest Phase 41 comparison; do not call it release-ready.
- Render unsmoothed training-loss and validation-loss points against optimizer step from retained
  raw events. A visually smoothed overlay is optional, but it must be labeled and may not replace
  the raw series. Metric tables, confusion matrices, curves, and efficiency plots carry source-log
  and model-artifact hashes.
- Before comparing LoRA with QLoRA, a machine-readable admissibility report must pass the matched
  configuration diff and genuine-QLoRA proof. PhoBERT is not claimed to have matched optimizer or
  tokenization because it is a different architecture; its fair comparison boundary is the same
  frozen data, label order, validation rows, seed declaration, and evaluator.
- The planned evidence is one full run per model at seed 42 because of the available compute and
  deadline. Report this single-run design as a limitation: it supports a measured case comparison,
  not a seed-robust ranking or statistical-significance claim.

## 6. Guardrails

Phase 40 has no user-facing production endpoint. Here, “online” means a fast guard that runs on
every experiment preflight, resume, validation prediction, or evidence-publication attempt. These
guards protect the later user-facing safety claim without pretending this training phase is
deployed.

### Online (Every Run / Prediction)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Train/validation allowlist | A supplied path is not the two canonical relative paths, resolves outside them, or a caller supplies a test/unrecognized split | Reject before opening the supplied path; emit a sanitized preflight error and create no run directory/model artifact |
| Frozen identity and lineage | Train/validation count or SHA differs, expected validation supports differ from 76/22/49/72, or any `seed_id` crosses train/validation | Abort before model loading; mark experiment invalid until the data contract is restored |
| Genuine QLoRA proof | QLoRA is requested but CUDA/bitsandbytes/`BitsAndBytesConfig`/NF4 proof fails, `is_loaded_in_4bit` is false, no `Linear4bit` modules exist, or adapter gradients are absent | Fail closed; never fall back to ordinary LoRA and never label the run QLoRA |
| Structured Qwen parse | Generated output is malformed, missing its label, contains an unknown label, produces multiple answers, or raises a parser error | Store raw output and exception as `invalid_output`; count it as an error and safety-gate failure, never as `benign` |
| Resume compatibility | A full-run checkpoint differs on split hashes, model revision, mode proof, formatter/preprocessor, label order, seeds, or optimizer/scheduler controls | Refuse resume and require a new run ID; probes are always non-resumable and non-publishable |
| Evidence publication | Raw log, resolved config, trainer state, predictions, graph source, checkpoint/adapter, or required hash is absent/invalid | Keep the run `incomplete`; do not publish it to the comparison registry or claim success |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Per-class precision/recall/F1 and macro F1 | Score all 219 validation rows at every declared evaluation checkpoint | Keep raw result; mark safety failure when a risky recall floor is missed; inspect the complete error slice before selecting a checkpoint |
| Dangerous-safe and invalid-output errors | Review 100% of risky→`benign`, risky→`invalid_output`, and all invalid outputs | Classify mechanism/parser cause; fix only code/config defects, never edit validation labels or hide the original prediction |
| Family-boundary and benign errors | Review all Zalo errors, all benign→risky errors, and bank↔Zalo↔task confusion cells; add a deterministic per-class sample of correct cases for calibration | Document recurring shortcut pattern; any training change starts a new run and is applied symmetrically to matched Qwen modes where relevant |
| Loss and optimization health | Analyze every raw train/eval event; no sampling | Stop/promote no checkpoint with NaN/Inf or missing curve provenance; diagnose divergence and retain failed-run evidence |
| Resource and timing evidence | Capture every probe step after warm-up and all full-run timing/VRAM summaries | Recompute ETA from raw timings; annotate hardware confounding; never backfill or hand-edit measurements |
| Artifact and graph integrity | Verify 100% of required files and hashes before registry publication and whenever evidence is copied from Colab | Quarantine incomplete/tampered bundles, regenerate graphs only from intact raw logs, and keep the failed verification report |
| Seed uncertainty | One matched seed (`42`) for the required runs; repeat only as a complete predeclared seed set | Always publish the single-run limitation; prohibit variance, t-test, significance, or stable-superiority claims until matched repeats exist |

## 7. Production Monitoring

Phase 40 monitors experiments rather than production traffic. User-facing monitoring is deferred to
a serving phase; the controls below must travel with any checkpoint selected for Phase 41.

**Tracing Tool:** Local append-only `events.jsonl`, raw prediction JSONL, resolved Pydantic evidence,
and Transformers `trainer_state.json`, all linked by run ID and SHA-256. Matplotlib consumes those
files read-only. Arize Phoenix is not configured for the offline-only reasons in Section 5.

**Key Metrics to Track:**

1. Frozen input identity: path allowlist result, train/validation counts and hashes, label supports,
   and cross-split seed overlap count.
2. Validation quality: macro/weighted F1, accuracy, per-class precision/recall/F1/support, the
   4×5 confusion matrix, risky-to-`benign` count, and `invalid_output` count/rate.
3. Training health: train/eval loss by optimizer step, learning rate, epoch, NaN/Inf count,
   selected checkpoint step, and early/interrupted/completed status.
4. Resource evidence: median post-warm-up step seconds, examples/s, tokens/s, peak allocated and
   reserved VRAM, eval/save overhead, actual wall time, projected local ETA, and exact hardware.
5. Reproducibility: matched-config diff, quantization proof, model/formatter/preprocessor revision,
   seed declaration, artifact/log/graph hashes, restart count, and evidence completeness status.

**Alert Thresholds:**

- **Immediate abort:** any non-allowlisted split request; input hash/count/support mismatch; non-zero
  train/validation seed overlap; incompatible resume; missing QLoRA proof; or attempted QLoRA→LoRA
  fallback.
- **Invalid run:** NaN/Inf loss, missing raw trace/trainer state/prediction rows, graph-source hash
  mismatch, artifact hash mismatch, fewer/more than 219 validation predictions, or duplicate/missing
  validation row IDs.
- **Safety-gate failure (report, do not conceal):** `bank_impersonation` recall <0.90,
  `zalo_social_engineering` recall <0.90, `task_scam` recall <0.80, or
  `invalid_output_count > 0`. The run may remain as negative experimental evidence but is not
  described as release-ready.
- **Comparison invalid:** any controlled Qwen field differs besides the declared base-weight
  quantization treatment. Different accelerator types do not invalidate quality metrics, but they
  suppress causal wall-time/throughput claims and require a hardware-confounded annotation.
- **Uncertainty warning:** fewer than two complete matched seeds (the planned state) forces the
  explicit single-run limitation and blocks significance or stability claims.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
Code evaluates all 219 rows; there is no metric subsampling. Human review is signal-weighted:
100% of invalid outputs and risky-to-`benign` errors; 100% of Zalo misclassifications because its
support is only 22; all benign-to-risky and cross-scam confusion cells; then a deterministic,
row-ID-hashed 10% sample of correct predictions per class to detect plausible-looking shortcut
behavior. Reviewer decisions and notes are append-only and never overwrite frozen labels or raw
model outputs.
